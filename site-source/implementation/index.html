01 Understand 02 Build 03 Hand over
01

understand

Get close
to the problem.

Walk through the real task with the people who do it. Look at the tools, handoffs, and constraints before deciding what should change.

Before a build

Agree on the problem, the intended result, the scope, and how the result will be checked.

A useful next step may be a process change, an existing tool, or a focused build.

02

build

Make it real.
Review it together.

Build a focused version and use working examples to review it. Test the normal path and the cases that are incomplete, unusual, or wrong.

Before expanding

Review what works, what needs correction, and what belongs outside the current scope.

Make limitations visible while decisions are still easy to change.

03

handover

Put the work
in capable hands.

Prepare the team to use the system. Document the workflow, responsibilities, limitations, and the checks needed after delivery.

Before handover

Agree on ownership, access, documentation, training, and any ongoing support.

Support is scoped explicitly. There should be no mystery about who owns the next step.

Which task keeps coming up?

Turning a new client’s email into a project brief.

What starts the work?

A new project request arrives by email. The project lead reads it before the first call.

Where does the work repeat?

The lead copies the goals, deadline and open questions into a separate document.

What will you try first?

Try a brief template on the next five requests. Use an AI draft to fill in details the client supplied and flag open questions.

Who makes the call?

The project lead checks the draft against the original email before sharing the brief.

How will you decide whether to keep it?

Compare preparation time with the previous five briefs. Record any details the lead must correct or add.
